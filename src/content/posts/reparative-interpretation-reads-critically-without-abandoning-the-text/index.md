I like to describe reparative reading as being curious, not judgemental. It does not deny the violence, ideology or harm that a text may contain. It resists deciding in advance that suspicion has exhausted everything the text can mean, and remains open to surprise, value and previously unheard voices.

Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick developed reparative reading as an alternative to making suspicious or “paranoid” reading the only legitimate critical posture. Helen Paynter applies this approach to Judges 19 in *Telling Terror in Judges 19*, acknowledging the story’s horror while attending to the woman’s prominence, agency and voice.
