The fact that some of the greatest threats to peace can emerge from religious groups is already anticipated in the biblical imagination. Scripture’s symbols of evil are not always things that look wholly unlike the kingdom of God. They often look strikingly similar: they promise order, deliverance, victory, abundance and a restored people, but introduce key distortions into each of them.

The resemblance is what makes the counterfeit persuasive. A strong ruler replaces the crucified king; domination replaces peace; national greatness replaces the reconciliation of all peoples; enemies are sacrificed rather than loved; and security is purchased through exclusion and violence. Evil borrows the symbols of salvation while reversing the way of Jesus.

This helps explain how the American evangelical right can support Donald Trump even when his character and politics appear to be the antithesis of Christian hope. If Christian hope has already been distorted into power, victory, protection and national restoration, Trump can appear not as a contradiction but as its fulfilment.
