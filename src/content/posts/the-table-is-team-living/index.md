Tables are places where ordinary life becomes communal: people eat, celebrate, tell stories, endure pain and make room for one another. Jesus repeatedly met people at tables, and his followers gather around the communion table to remember his self-giving love. The image therefore resists an individualistic account of discipleship.

The church is not merely an audience watching experts perform religious activity. It is a family and learning community in which every person contributes, receives care and grows in the capacity to care for others. Hospitality must move beyond polite inclusion toward genuine shared life: making room for difference, bearing burdens, forgiving one another and allowing people to be known.
