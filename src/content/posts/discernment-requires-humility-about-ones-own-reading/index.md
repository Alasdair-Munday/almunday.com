Wisdom can be found in surprising places. Interpretation should remain open to correction by the wider community, especially when dealing with symbolic narratives and contested theological claims. Other readers may have seen something we have missed, particularly when they stand outside our usual interpretive community.

It is wrong to dismiss interpretations from other sources before allowing them to challenge our own. Discernment is not the defence of a private reading, but the humble testing of what we think we know.

This includes the wisdom carried by people whose belonging is re-situated by divine love. To be named and loved by God can mean being misunderstood by the world, because divine love gives people a different social location. Those whom dominant communities overlook may see truths that insiders have learned not to notice.

Proverbs itself models this openness. It draws on surrounding ancient Near Eastern wisdom traditions and adapts them within its theological vision. Biblical wisdom is not presented as culturally isolated; it can recognise truth beyond Israel while re-situating that truth in relation to YHWH.
