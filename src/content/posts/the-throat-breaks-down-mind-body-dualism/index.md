The throat is a connecting point between breath, body, voice and self. It unsettles mind–body dualism because thought and spirit become audible only through an irreducibly physical act: breath passing through flesh.

The wisdom of Tibetan Buddhist throat singing lies partly in this embodied integration. Contemplation is not treated as something that happens inside a mind detached from the body; breath, vibration, disciplined attention and communal sound belong to a single spiritual practice.

This resonates with the Hebrew word *nephesh*, which lies behind much of the Bible’s language of “soul.” Its concrete range of meaning includes throat, appetite, breath, life, person and living being. The biblical soul is therefore not best imagined as an immaterial object trapped inside a body. The *nephesh* is the living, breathing, desiring self—and the throat is one place where that unity becomes perceptible.
