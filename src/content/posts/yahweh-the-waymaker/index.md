If a friend hurts you, should you tell a grown-up or hurt them back?

You tell a grown-up. You take the situation to the person with authority rather than trying to settle it through revenge.

Then, while the grown-up speaks to your friend, should you stand beside them shaking your fist? No. You leave the person with authority to do their job and go somewhere else.

It is a childish example, but it exposes an adult temptation. When something is wrong, we want to make it right in our own way. Even after placing it in God’s hands, we can remain beside him metaphorically shaking our fists, anxious that he will not act properly without our help. We reach for the solution that looks most plausible instead of trusting God to keep his promises.

That is the tension at the heart of the Red Sea story.

## God leads Israel into a trap

God tells the Israelites exactly where to camp:

> “Tell the Israelites to turn back and encamp near Pi Hahiroth, between Migdol and the sea. They are to encamp by the sea, directly opposite Baal Zephon.”

These details matter. *Migdol* means “tower.” It was probably part of the Egyptian line of fortifications known as the Way of Horus. If you were trying to escape Egypt, camping between its defences and the sea was not an obviously sensible plan.

Baal Zephon was associated with a Canaanite storm god believed to wield power over the sea. Israel is trapped with an Egyptian military position on one side, the sea on another, and a shrine representing an apparent alternative source of deliverance nearby. Then Pharaoh’s army approaches.

The gods and powers of the world seem to be calling Israel away from Yahweh at precisely the moment when trusting Yahweh appears most foolish.

God has led them there deliberately.

Why?

Because God has swagger.

Swagger can mean the attitude of somebody with a boastful, stylish and profoundly self-assured personality. God is more self-assured than anyone we can imagine because God knows exactly who he is. He is the waymaker, the miracle worker, the light in the darkness—the one who makes highways through the sea.

The plagues have already revealed Yahweh’s authority over the gods of Egypt. At the shore of the sea, God prepares to reveal it again.

## The temptation to return to Egypt

Israel’s response is wonderfully sarcastic:

> “Was it because there were no graves in Egypt that you brought us to the desert to die?”

They tell Moses that slavery would have been better than death in the wilderness. Egypt suddenly looks safe because it is familiar. When fear closes in, even captivity can acquire a nostalgic glow.

This is the Baal Zephon temptation. The powers, leaders and systems around us promise that they can save us if we will only return our loyalty to them. But the kings of this world are not our ultimate salvation. The gods of Egypt have already been exposed as powerless. Israel’s hope is in Yahweh, the God who keeps his promises.

With Pharaoh behind them and the sea ahead, Moses says:

> “The Lord will fight for you; you need only be still.”

Stillness does not mean that Israel will never move. In a moment, they will walk through the sea. It means that their rescue begins with God’s action, not their frantic attempts to manufacture an escape.

## Creation, de-creation and re-creation

God is not merely the one who created long ago. God is always the creator. God has created, God is creating, and God will make all things new.

The separation and collapse of waters forms a pattern throughout the Bible. In Genesis 1, the waters represent the uninhabitable chaos of non-creation. God separates them, dry land appears and life becomes possible. In the flood, the waters collapse back in and creation is undone. De-creation occurs when the order that sustains life gives way again to chaos.

At the Red Sea, God repeats the creative act. The water separates. Dry land appears. Israel walks through the chaos into freedom.

Everything is reversed. The sea, which had looked like certain death, becomes the safe road. The military power that had looked so secure enters that road and is overwhelmed. What appeared to be land proves chaotic, and what appeared to be chaos becomes the place of salvation.

God is making a new creation. Israel passes through the waters and emerges on the other side as a liberated people, called into covenant and towards the promised land.

## Baptised through the sea

Paul describes Israel’s passage through the sea as a kind of baptism. Baptism calls us to turn away from all the powers and promises we imagine might save us and to entrust ourselves to God instead.

We pass through the water because Jesus has passed through death.

Jesus entered the deepest chaos, died and came out the other side into resurrection life. The Red Sea is therefore not merely a dramatic rescue from a long time ago. It gives us the shape of Christian salvation: God makes a way through death into life, through captivity into freedom and through chaos into new creation.

This does not mean that every impossible situation will be resolved in the way or at the time we hope.

My wife Jess and I both felt called to ordained ministry. That is not the most straightforward calling for a couple with a young family. During training we spoke to different dioceses and parts of the Church of England, and at times the process became extremely difficult. We wondered how we could move towards the place God appeared to be calling us when there seemed to be a sea in the way. I could always return to software development; that had been quite nice, after all.

By God’s grace, Sheffield found a helpful and creative way forward. God made a way we could not see.

There was another time, shortly before our daughter Iris was born, when Jess’s waters broke very early. She went into hospital, and for weeks we felt completely at sea. There was almost nothing we could do. We could only wait.

God brought us through that too.

But sometimes the path through is not visible in the here and now. Some stories do not reach a tidy resolution within this life. Christian hope is not a technique for forcing God to give us the outcome we want. It is trust in the creator who has promised to make all things new.

The waters will be separated. In Christ, a way has already been opened through them.

Where do you feel trapped between the tower, the false promise of Baal Zephon, an approaching army and the sea? Where are you tempted either to return to captivity or to manufacture your own escape?

Be still. Let God be God. Then, when the way opens, walk through it.

Yahweh is the one who makes a way through the sea.
