Revelation destabilises the assumption that victory means becoming a stronger Caesar. John hears of a conquering Lion and sees a Lamb standing as though slaughtered. The Lamb is worthy to receive power, wealth, wisdom, strength, honour, glory and praise precisely because he was slain.

Philippians describes the same shape of divine victory. Christ makes himself nothing, takes the form of a servant and becomes obedient to death on a cross; therefore God exalts him and gives him the name above every name. His humiliation is not an unfortunate prelude to a different, more conventional form of power. It reveals what the glory and power of God are actually like.

Christlike conquest is therefore self-giving, faithful witness, solidarity and life offered for others—not preservation of the self through the sacrifice of others.
