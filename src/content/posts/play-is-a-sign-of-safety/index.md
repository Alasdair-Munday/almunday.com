**The Vision:** Zechariah envisions a city marked by total security and peace—a place where the vulnerable (the elderly and children) thrive, and where joyful play is the evidence of safety. Play is not a trivial distraction; it is a profound foretaste of the Kingdom of God.

**The Problem:** In our fallen world, adult life is dominated by performance, defensive posturing, anxiety, and self-preservation. We build relational trenches out of fear, pride, and the pressure to produce.

**The Gospel Freedom:** Jesus invites us to receive the Kingdom like little children (Mark 10:14–15). His finished work secures our identity, freeing us from the need to prove ourselves and allowing us to step out of our defensive postures into honest, peaceful fellowship.

**The Spirit’s Empowerment:** The Spirit produces joy, peace, and mutual delight, creating a community where we can rest and play without shame or competition.

Peace = secure, confident, undefended

**Passages to explore:** Zechariah 8:4–5; Mark 10:13–16; John 15:9–15; Romans 8:14–16.

## Question to develop

How might receiving the Kingdom of God “like a little child” change our approach to community?
