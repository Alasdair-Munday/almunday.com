> “The world will never starve for want of wonders; but only for want of wonder.”
>
> — G. K. Chesterton, *Tremendous Trifles*

Chesterton’s claim connects with the biblical fear of the Lord. Wisdom begins with an attentive, reverent openness to God and to the world as gift, rather than with mastery or the accumulation of information.

William P. Brown develops this connection in *Wisdom’s Wonder*, arguing that wonder provides a framework for biblical wisdom and its formation of character. The fear of the Lord can therefore be understood as wonder becoming a posture of receptive attention, contemplation and faithful action.
