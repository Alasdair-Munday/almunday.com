The Hebrew term *na’ar* is often translated “child,” but carries much more meaning. Its precise sense is highly debated, and many scholars understand it ordinarily to refer to a young man. Some scholarship connects the term with the transition between childhood and adulthood: *na’ar* commonly identifies someone in a liminal moment, whose previous chapter has ended while their future remains uncertain.

In Proverbs, I think the *na’ar* belongs within an adolescent phase. This person has begun to move beyond the *peti*—the simple or inexperienced person—but has not yet decisively chosen the way of either wisdom or folly. Reading *na’ar* merely as “child” obscures this moment of formation and decision.
